The hard part of most products isn't the code — it's the moment someone has to figure out what to do next. That's the part I like working on.

So I build things that are hard to explain and make them easy to use: <Reveal project="how-nigeria-works">a 3D guide to Nigeria's government</Reveal> where every claim cites the constitution, <Reveal project="california-housing-intelligence">a housing-price model</Reveal> you can poke at, and <Reveal project="careerfit">a career recommender</Reveal> that gets the right answer in its top three 94% of the time.

As a frontend engineer on the DevFest Lagos team, I've worked on <Reveal project="devfest-lagos-2024">the 2024 site</Reveal>, where accessibility went from 82% to 96%, and <Reveal project="devfest-lagos-2025-ticketing">the 2025 ticketing</Reveal>, where one person can buy for a whole group.

I build in public and ship small and often. I'm open to frontend and full-stack roles, ideally on a product whose hardest problem is making something complicated feel simple.
